# Experience through motion

Design proposal · 18 November 2026 · Awaiting design review; no website changes made

## The intended experience

A visitor should be able to describe Harith's contribution after watching each short sequence: coordinating plant work, connecting property verification teams, and turning research into leadership documents. The visual should carry the action; a few words identify who did what. Longer explanations, evidence and metrics remain available for deeper reading.

The current scenes identify the setting, but floating plant layers and moving paper do not sufficiently explain the work. This revision gives every movement a purpose: assign, connect, compare, resolve, assemble, or deliver.

Keep the existing cream, amber, forest and rust palette, editorial typography and chapter order. Use a wide visual stage occupying approximately 70% of each desktop chapter. Place the company identity, role and a single contribution sentence above it. Put a compact three-step strip below it, followed by an accessible “Explore my work” disclosure containing the existing detail. Essential role information stays visible even with that disclosure closed.

## Chapter 1 — Ethanol / Coordinate the moving parts

**What the viewer should understand:** “I coordinated civil, mechanical and clearance workstreams to support plant commissioning readiness.”

**First view:** an assembled ethanol plant above a compact project schedule. Three clearly labelled workstream lanes lead into it. A distinct coordination node labelled “My role” connects the lanes. This makes the project-management responsibility visible before the first scroll.

| Beat | Visible action | Short caption | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Plan | A schedule unfolds; civil, mechanical and clearance tasks connect to the relevant plant areas. | Sequence the work | Planning across dependent workstreams |
| 2. Coordinate | A delivery marker pauses at a dependency. An issue card connects to the relevant team; the sequence updates and the handoff continues. | Resolve the handoffs | Team/vendor coordination and bottleneck management |
| 3. Review readiness | Workstream evidence enters a readiness checklist beside the assembled plant; the remaining-review items stay distinguishable. | Track commissioning readiness | Progress visibility and coordinated readiness review |

**Objects:** recognizable tanks and piping, civil foundations, a small delivery vehicle/crate, schedule bars, milestone markers, issue card, clearance folder and checklist. All schedules and issue examples are illustrative. Do not animate a successful plant start-up or regulatory approval as an achieved result.

**Selection continuity:** retain Overview / Civil / Mechanical / Clearances. The same three beats adapt to the chosen workstream. Civil highlights site preparation, readiness and installation handoffs; mechanical highlights equipment delivery, interfaces and vendor coordination; clearances highlights document requirements, follow-up and review status. Inactive work remains assembled and subdued. Changing selection preserves the current progress and camera position. Reverse scroll reverses the same sequence.

**Deep dive:** role/dates, résumé-supported project scale and results, responsibilities and a note that the plant is illustrative rather than as-built. Metrics remain static, correctly labelled résumé claims rather than simulated live readings.

## Chapter 2 — LandSafe / Bring the checks together

**What the viewer should understand:** “I co-founded LandSafe and coordinated property due diligence, from records and field checks to a structured report.”

**First view:** original LandSafe logo, fictional parcel, three specialist lanes labelled Records / Field & GIS / Legal review, and a report outline. The logo also appears on the report cover, making this recognizably LandSafe rather than a generic property scene.

| Beat | Visible action | Short caption | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Organize | Ownership, revenue and encumbrance record cards connect into an evidence chain; a missing-record slot remains visible. | Organize the evidence | Record sourcing and follow-up |
| 2. Cross-check | A survey instrument and observation markers reveal a second outline above the fictional parcel. A discrepancy points to a review card. The legal lane remains separate. | Coordinate the checks | Connecting field, GIS and legal evidence |
| 3. Deliver | The three lanes feed a categorized review matrix, then assemble into a LandSafe-branded property report with findings and next-action tabs. | Deliver a clearer report | Synthesis and service delivery |

The discrepancy does not magically become “clear.” It remains a flagged question within the report. Never use an “Approved,” “Safe to buy,” or government-style seal. A completed report is not a clean-title guarantee.

**Business contribution:** a small secondary sequence inside the deep dive connects enquiry → scope → specialist coordination → report delivery. This represents customer acquisition and service operations without crowding the primary due-diligence story.

**Deep dive:** co-founder/operations scope, 30+ parameter framework, aggregate résumé results and the anonymized method summary. Preserve the distinction between coordinating specialist work and personally performing every legal or surveying task.

## Motion and interaction rules

- **One driver on desktop:** chapter scroll progress determines the three beats. Stage buttons navigate to the matching beat anchor. There is no timer running against the scroll. A visitor can move directly to any beat or continue to the next chapter.
- **Short chapters:** start with approximately 1.5–2 viewport heights of scroll travel per chapter, then adjust after viewing. No forced wait or completion gate. First and final poses must explain the work when someone scrolls quickly.
- **Responsive motion:** target 200–350 ms for selected-state transitions, 350–500 ms for object assembly, and brief 40–60 ms stagger where it helps reading. Scroll-linked motion responds directly, with only light smoothing. These are design targets, not measured performance claims.
- **Camera discipline:** stable three-quarter view for plant and parcel; mostly frontal editorial composition for research. Small push-ins may emphasize a handoff; no continuous rotation or drifting objects.
- **Visible cause and effect:** a task connects to a team, a field observation joins a record, or research becomes a briefing. Decorative movement must not compete with these actions.
- **Mobile:** three compact vertically stacked storyboard beats; simple entry transitions and direct stage anchors. No long pinned scene or tiny slider. Keep each action and caption visible together.
- **Reduced motion / no WebGL:** static keyframes with arrows and short action captions retain the same meaning. Keyboard controls, focus states and accessible summaries remain available. Avoid announcing every scroll frame to screen readers.
- **Color meaning:** amber identifies active work, forest identifies organized/completed preparation, rust identifies an unresolved issue. Pair each meaning with a label or symbol; color alone does not communicate status.

## Implementation sequence after design review

1. **Ground the assets.** Inspect and prepare the supplied LandSafe mark; verify a public Telangana outline and its reuse terms; confirm every depicted action against the résumé and safe source summaries. Do not upload private documents to an image service. External reference art will not be copied into the portfolio.
2. **Build one representative sequence.** Start with LandSafe's records → field comparison → report sequence. Review its three still poses and transition before repeating the approach. This checks whether the visual communicates the contribution.
3. **Adapt the existing scenes.** Reuse the plant and renderer setup. Add project-management overlays; replace research's generic paper motion with the map-to-brief sequence. Use SVG/HTML for labels, schedules and document content, and Three.js where spatial relationships improve understanding. No new animation dependency is planned.
4. **Rebalance the chapter layout.** Enlarge the visual stage; reduce visible copy to role, one contribution sentence and action captions. Move the existing substantive detail into disclosures with working deep links. Preserve the homepage, products, About, books and retro-printer résumé.
5. **Verify meaning and behavior.** Inspect first/middle/final frames, reverse scrolling, stage navigation, plant filter continuity, mobile, reduced motion and WebGL fallback. Check identifiers, claims and asset provenance, then run TypeScript/build and targeted browser checks.

Likely edit scope: `PremiumPortfolio.tsx`, `PlantScene.tsx`, `ArtifactScene.tsx`, `EvidenceChapters.tsx`, their associated styles, safe brand/map assets and targeted verification scripts. No broad refactor, new route or deployment is needed.

## Acceptance criteria

- With long descriptions closed, each sequence shows the input, Harith's action and the deliverable using pictures and brief labels.
- A first-time viewer can explain the contribution after one pass. This requires a human comprehension check; passing automated tests does not prove it.
- Ethanol reads as project coordination, LandSafe as coordinated due diligence, and political research as analysis/briefing work.
- Real LandSafe identity is present; no invented company marks, fabricated outcomes or client identifiers appear.
- Selecting Civil never brings mechanical or clearance actions into the foreground; equivalent isolation holds for the other workstreams.
- No compulsory playback, scroll trap, visual jump when changing filters, overlapping caption, horizontal overflow or missing reduced-motion content.
- Transitions are measured on desktop and phone-sized viewports after implementation; rendering performance is checked before adding further detail.

Guidance applied: Karpathy guidelines for narrow scope and verifiable outcomes; 3Dviz Pro Max's subject-clarity, meaningful-motion and design-synthesis guidance. No scene catalog kit was retrieved or claimed as used.
